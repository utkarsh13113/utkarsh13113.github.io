01 / About

Still figuring a few things out

I'm Utkarsh, a Class 12 student from Delhi NCR, currently working through the PCM stream and preparing for JEE 2027. I'm not going to pretend I've got it all figured out — I'm still deciding exactly what I want to specialize in. Right now, electronics, electrical engineering and semiconductor technology interest me the most.

I like understanding how things work underneath — how a circuit actually does what it does, how a chip goes from a design file to a working piece of silicon. That curiosity is what this site is really about. Long term, I'd like to work in the semiconductor or chip industry, maybe somewhere like NVIDIA or Google — that's an aspiration I'm working toward, not something I've done yet.

Outside of academics, I spend a fair amount of time thinking about future plans and keeping backup options open, organizing whatever I'm working on, and keeping track of money and how I'm spending it. None of that is a "skill" in a formal sense — it's just how I tend to operate.

07 / Curiosity Board

Things I'm Curious About

Questions I'm sitting with right now — not claims of expertise. This board will keep growing.

How do modern semiconductor chips actually work?

Why I'm curious
Almost everything I use runs on one, but the actual physics-to-computation link is still a black box to me.
What I currently understand
The very basics — transistors act as switches, and billions of them combine to do logic.
What I want to learn next
How transistor-level switching turns into actual instructions being executed.

How does electrical engineering connect to chip design?

Why I'm curious
I want to know which parts of core electrical engineering actually show up in chip design work.
What I currently understand
Very little beyond the fact that circuits and signal behaviour are involved.
What I want to learn next
Where circuit theory ends and chip-specific design begins.

How are billions of transistors manufactured?

Why I'm curious
The scale is hard to picture — manufacturing precision at that level seems almost impossible.
What I currently understand
Almost nothing concrete yet, beyond the general term "fabrication."
What I want to learn next
What a fabrication process actually looks like, step by step.

How do GPUs become capable of massive parallel computation?

Why I'm curious
I keep hearing "parallel processing" but don't yet understand what makes GPU architecture suited to it.
What I currently understand
That GPUs have many smaller cores compared to a CPU's fewer, more powerful ones.
What I want to learn next
How a task actually gets split across those cores.

How does hardware interact with software?

Why I'm curious
The gap between code and physical circuits feels like the most interesting part of computing.
What I currently understand
The rough idea of layers — software eventually becomes instructions hardware can run.
What I want to learn next
What actually happens in between, layer by layer.

What skills actually matter before entering engineering college?

Why I'm curious
I want to spend my remaining school time on things that will actually help, not just look good.
What I currently understand
That fundamentals in math and physics probably matter more than any single tool.
What I want to learn next
Which specific habits or basics tend to make the first year of engineering college easier.
